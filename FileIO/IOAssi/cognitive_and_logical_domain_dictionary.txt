# Domain Overview: Cognitive Psychology and Informal Logic

This document defines the processes, key structures, and vocabulary used to categorize and understand human errors in judgment, memory, and argumentation.

## 1. Key Structures and Language

The domain is organized hierarchically, moving from broad classifications to specific manifestations:

*   **Domain:** The highest level of classification, splitting the concepts into either **Cognitive Biases** (psychological tendencies and mental shortcuts) or **Logical Fallacies** (flaws in the structure or premise of an argument).
*   **Category:** Broad groupings based on the area of human experience or reasoning affected (e.g., Decision-making bias, Fallacy of Relevance).
*   **Core Mechanism:** The underlying psychological or logical process occurring in the brain or in the argument structure (e.g., "Filtering and assimilating new data points").
*   **Primary Drivers:** The fundamental human motivations or logical missteps that trigger the core mechanism (e.g., Ego preservation, Pattern-seeking behavior).
*   **Sub-type:** A more granular classification of the specific bias or fallacy (e.g., Heuristic shortcut, Goalpost moving).
*   **Manifestation (The Specific Term):** The recognized name of the bias or fallacy as it appears in real-world scenarios (e.g., Sunk Cost Fallacy, Straw Man).

## 2. Process Definitions (Core Mechanisms)

These are the active processes and cognitive shortcuts that lead to the biases and fallacies defined in the dictionary:

*   **Active selection between options under uncertainty:** Relies on immediate situational framing rather than long-term utility calculation. Driven by risk aversion, reward seeking, and inertia.
*   **Evaluating human behavior and intent (self vs. others):** Assumes internal flaws in others and external obstacles for oneself. Driven by tribalism, ego preservation, and social conformity.
*   **Distorting past recall or future probability estimates:** Substitutes "ease of recall" for "statistical probability." Driven by emotional impact, recent exposure, and survival instincts.
*   **Filtering and assimilating new data points:** Accepts confirming evidence easily while subjecting contrary evidence to intense scrutiny. Driven by cognitive dissonance avoidance.
*   **Substituting logical evidence with unrelated, emotionally charged variables:** The argument's conclusion does not logically follow from its premises. Driven by deflection and appeals to emotion/authority.
*   **Drawing sweeping conclusions from inadequate or cherry-picked data:** The sample size or data selection is insufficient to prove causation. Driven by pattern-seeking and overgeneralization.
*   **Baking the desired conclusion directly into the foundational premise:** Bypasses the need for actual proof by establishing an artificially constrained framework. Driven by circular reasoning and false binaries.
*   **Exploiting linguistic loopholes, double meanings, or semantic shifts:** Relies on the deception of terminology rather than the substance of the claim. Driven by semantic manipulation.

## 3. Dictionary of Domain Terms (A-Z)

### A
*   **Ad Hominem (Fallacy):** Attacking an opponent's character or personal traits in an attempt to undermine their argument.
*   **Ad hominem attacks (Driver):** A conversational tactic targeting character or motive rather than the substance of the argument.
*   **Anchoring Bias (Bias):** Relying too heavily on the first piece of information offered (the 'anchor') when making decisions.
*   **Appeal to Authority (Fallacy):** Asserting a claim is true simply because an authority said it was true, without supporting evidence.
*   **Appeal to authority or emotion (Driver):** Exploiting audience feelings or citing unqualified authorities to bypass logical evidence.
*   **Appeal to Emotion (Fallacy):** Manipulating an emotional response in place of a valid or compelling argument.
*   **Appeal to Ignorance (Fallacy):** Arguing that a proposition must be true because it has not been proven false, or vice versa.
*   **Appeal to Nature (Fallacy):** Arguing that because something is 'natural' it is therefore valid, justified, good, or ideal.
*   **Availability Heuristic (Bias):** Overestimating the importance or likelihood of events based on how easily examples come to mind.

### B
*   **Bandwagon / Ad Populum (Fallacy):** Appealing to popularity or the fact that many people do something as an attempted form of validation.
*   **Bandwagon Effect (Bias):** The tendency to do or believe things because many other people do or believe the same.
*   **Begging the Question / Circular Reasoning (Fallacy):** A circular argument in which the conclusion is included in the premise.
*   **Blind-Spot Bias (Bias):** Recognizing the impact of biases on the judgment of others, while failing to see the impact on one's own judgment.
*   **Burden of Proof (Fallacy):** Asserting that the burden of proof lies not with the person making the claim, but with someone else to disprove.

### C
*   **Choice-Supportive Bias (Bias):** The tendency to remember one's choices as better than they actually were.
*   **Circular reasoning (Driver):** A logical flaw where the premise simply restates the conclusion rather than proving it.
*   **Cognitive Bias (Domain):** Systematic patterns of deviation from norm and rationality in judgment.
*   **Cognitive dissonance avoidance (Driver):** The mental discomfort from holding contradictory beliefs, leading to the rejection of new, conflicting information.
*   **Confirmation Bias (Bias):** The tendency to search for, interpret, focus on, and remember information in a way that confirms one's preconceptions.

### D
*   **Decision-making bias (Category):** Errors in judgment when making choices, often driven by heuristics, emotional attachments, or perceived losses/gains.
*   **Decoy Effect (Bias):** A change in preference between two options when presented with a third option that is asymmetrically dominated.
*   **Deflection (Driver):** Redirecting criticism or focus away from the original issue or oneself onto another topic or person.
*   **Desire for consistency (Driver):** The need to maintain alignment between past behaviors and current beliefs, even when proven wrong.
*   **Dunning-Kruger Effect (Bias):** A bias in which people with low ability at a task overestimate their ability.

### E
*   **Ego preservation (Driver):** The unconscious defense mechanism aimed at protecting self-esteem from threats or perceived failures.
*   **Emotional impact (Driver):** The degree to which an event evokes strong feelings, heavily skewing memory and perceived importance.
*   **Endowment Effect (Bias):** The tendency for people to demand much more to give up an object than they would pay to acquire it.
*   **Equivocation (Fallacy):** Using a double meaning or ambiguity of language to mislead or misrepresent the truth.
*   **Evolutionary survival instincts (Driver):** Deep-seated biological drives that often misfire in modern, non-lethal scenarios.

### F
*   **Fallacy of Ambiguity (Category):** Arguments that manipulate language, shifting definitions mid-argument to confuse or mislead.
*   **Fallacy of Defective Induction (Category):** Arguments drawing sweeping conclusions from inadequate or cherry-picked data.
*   **Fallacy of Presumption (Category):** Arguments relying on an unjustified assumption or a premise that assumes the conclusion is true.
*   **Fallacy of Relevance (Category):** Arguments where premises are logically irrelevant to the conclusion, relying on emotional appeal or distraction.
*   **False binaries (Driver):** Artificially reducing a complex spectrum of possibilities down to only two mutually exclusive options.
*   **False Cause / Post Hoc (Fallacy):** Presuming that a real or perceived relationship between things means that one is the cause of the other.
*   **False Dilemma / Black-or-White (Fallacy):** Presenting two alternative states as the only possibilities, when more possibilities exist.
*   **Framing Effect (Bias):** Drawing different conclusions from the same information, depending on how that information is presented.
*   **Fundamental Attribution Error (Bias):** Over-emphasizing personality-based explanations for others' behaviors while under-emphasizing situational ones.

### G
*   **Gambler's Fallacy (Fallacy):** Believing that 'runs' occur to statistically independent phenomena such as roulette wheel spins.
*   **Genetic Fallacy (Fallacy):** Judging something good or bad on the basis of where it comes from, or from whom it comes.
*   **Goalpost moving (Driver):** Demanding stronger evidence or changing the criteria for proof after the initial criteria have been met.

### H
*   **Halo Effect (Bias):** The tendency for positive impressions in one area to positively influence one's opinion or feelings in other areas.
*   **Hasty Generalization (Fallacy):** Drawing a conclusion based on a small sample size rather than typical statistics.
*   **Hindsight Bias (Bias):** The tendency to see past events as being predictable at the time those events happened.

### I
*   **In-Group Favoritism (Bias):** Giving preferential treatment to others perceived to be members of one's own group.
*   **Inertia (Driver):** The preference for the current state or default option, resisting change due to required effort.
*   **Information Processing & Belief bias (Category):** Biases affecting how people seek out, interpret, and integrate information.

### L
*   **Logical Fallacy (Domain):** Flaws in reasoning that undermine the logical validity of an argument.
*   **Loss Aversion (Bias):** The tendency to prefer avoiding losses to acquiring equivalent gains.

### M
*   **Memory & Probability bias (Category):** Biases that affect how people encode, retrieve, or forecast information and likelihoods.
*   **Middle Ground (Fallacy):** Claiming that a compromise, or middle point, between two extremes must be the truth.

### N
*   **Negativity Bias (Bias):** Phenomenon by which humans have a greater recall of unpleasant memories compared with positive ones.
*   **No True Scotsman (Fallacy):** Making an appeal to purity as a way to dismiss relevant criticisms or flaws of an argument.

### O
*   **Optimism Bias (Bias):** The tendency to be over-optimistic, overestimating favorable outcomes.
*   **Ostrich Effect (Bias):** Avoiding exposing oneself to information that one fears may cause psychological discomfort.
*   **Overgeneralization (Driver):** Drawing broad, sweeping conclusions from a single event or a very small, unrepresentative sample.

### P
*   **Pattern-seeking behavior (Driver):** The human tendency to find meaningful connections or patterns in random or unrelated data.
*   **Personal Incredulity (Fallacy):** Concluding that because something is difficult to understand, it is therefore not true.
*   **Placebo Effect (Bias):** When simply believing that something will have a certain effect on you causes it to have that effect.

### R
*   **Recent exposure (Driver):** The cognitive shortcut where recently acquired information disproportionately influences judgment.
*   **Red Herring (Fallacy):** Introducing an irrelevant topic to divert attention from the original issue.
*   **Reward seeking (Driver):** The psychological drive to pursue positive outcomes, sometimes at the expense of rational risk assessment.
*   **Risk aversion (Driver):** Preferring a sure outcome over a gamble with higher or equal expected value.

### S
*   **Self-Serving Bias (Bias):** The tendency to claim more responsibility for successes than failures.
*   **Semantic manipulation (Driver):** Deliberate distortion, shifting, or ambiguous use of words to mislead an opponent.
*   **Slippery Slope (Fallacy):** Asserting that if A is allowed to happen, then Z will consequently happen, therefore A should not happen.
*   **Social & Attribution bias (Category):** Systematic patterns of deviation in judgment regarding inferences about other people or groups.
*   **Social conformity (Driver):** Matching attitudes and behaviors to group norms to fit in or gain approval.
*   **Status Quo Bias (Bias):** The tendency to like things to stay relatively the same, perceiving change as a loss.
*   **Straw Man (Fallacy):** Misrepresenting, exaggerating, or fabricating someone's argument to make it easier to attack.
*   **Sunk Cost Fallacy (Bias):** Continuing an endeavor once an investment has been made, rather than cutting losses.
*   **Survivorship Bias (Bias):** Concentrating on things that 'survived' a process and inadvertently overlooking those that didn't.

### T
*   **Texas Sharpshooter (Fallacy):** Cherry-picking data clusters to suit an argument, or finding a pattern to fit a presumption.
*   **Tribalism (Driver):** The tendency to align with and favor one's own in-group while distrusting the out-group.
*   **Tu Quoque (Fallacy):** Answering criticism with criticism; turning it back on the accuser.
